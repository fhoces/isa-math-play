05 -- A shape hunt

The idea. Shapes and symmetry are everywhere once you look. Today we go find them, photograph them, and sort them. This is the whole course out in the wild.

You need: the house and the outdoors, a camera or phone (optional), paper and pencil, maybe a basket or box for sorting little finds. Time: 20 to 30 minutes.

Game 1: the shape hunt

Go room to room (and outside if you can) and find real shapes hiding in things.

  1. Find circles: a plate, a clock, a wheel, a coin, a button.
  2. Find triangles: a slice of pizza, a roof, a yield sign, a coat hanger.
  3. Find rectangles: a door, a book, a window, a phone, a brick.
  4. Find hexagons (six sides): a honeycomb, a bolt head, a floor tile, a pencil seen end-on.

Make a tally as you go: one mark for each shape you spot.

Ask her: which shape did we find the most of? Which was the hardest to find?

Game 2: the symmetry hunt

Now hunt for things with a line of symmetry, a fold line where one half matches the other. Remember the mirror from Module 01.

  • Faces, leaves, butterflies, forks, windows, the front of a car.
  • Stand a finger (or a real mirror) down the middle to check: do the halves match?

Ask her: what is the most symmetric thing you can find? (A plate or a wheel has endless fold lines, every line through the center works.)

Game 3: the tiling hunt

Find a real tiling: a surface covered with shapes and no gaps in between.

  • A tile floor, a brick wall, a chessboard, a honeycomb, a soccer ball.
  • Look at the one shape that repeats to fill it. Bricks are rectangles. A chessboard is squares. A honeycomb is hexagons.

Ask her: are there any gaps between the pieces? (A good tiling has none. That is the whole trick from Module 02.)

Game 4: sort your finds

Photograph or draw what you found. Then sort the pictures into piles: a circle pile, a triangle pile, a rectangle pile, a hexagon pile. Put the symmetric ones together. Hang the best ones up.

Ask her: can one thing go in two piles? (A window can be a rectangle and symmetric. Lots of things wear more than one shape.)

Notice together (no pressure)

  • Shapes are not just in math books; they are in the floor, the fruit bowl, and the sky. Once you see them you cannot stop.
  • The honeycomb's hexagon is the same six-sided shape as the snowflake's six from Module 01, and the same hexagon Ben builds with a compass.
  • A brick wall and a tile floor are real tilings, the no-gap filling from Module 02.
  • Symmetry, tiling, and shapes were the whole course. Today you found them all hiding in plain sight.

For the grown-up

  • This is a review and a celebration, not a test. There are no right answers and no score; a shrug at any question is fine.
  • Let the connections land lightly: the honeycomb hexagon ties back to the snowflake (Module 01) and forward to Ben's compass hexagon; the brick wall and tile floor are the tilings of Module 02. You do not need to lecture; just point and say "hey, that is our hexagon again."
  • Keep the camera optional. If photos are stressful, drawing the finds works just as well and slows the looking down.
  • Rainy day with no outdoors? Mathigon Polypad (see ../resources.md) is full of shapes to find, sort, and tile on screen.
