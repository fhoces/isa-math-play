03 -- Patterns and what comes next

The idea. When you find the pattern, you can guess what comes next without building the whole thing. Finding that rule is the first taste of math magic: you can jump ahead.

You need: anything you have a bunch of in two or three kinds: beads, blocks, buttons, pasta, coins, stickers, or just a pencil to draw shapes. Time: 20 to 30 minutes.

Game 1: patterns that repeat

Lay out a row that repeats, like blue, red, blue, red, blue, red. Or stamp shapes: circle, square, circle, square. Or do sounds: clap, snap, clap, snap.

  1. Start a repeating row together. Keep it short at first.
  2. Stop in the middle and let her add the next few.
  3. Try a longer repeat: red, red, blue, then red, red, blue again. Or three colors: green, yellow, orange, green, yellow, orange.

Ask her: what comes next? How did you know? (The answer is "because it repeats." That is the rule.)

Ask her: what is the 10th one, without pointing to each? For a repeat of two (blue, red), every even spot is red, so the 10th is red. Count by the repeat, do not count one at a time. This is the magic: you can skip ahead.

Game 2: patterns that grow

Now make a row where each step is a little bigger than the last.

  1. Staircase. Build steps of blocks: 1 block, then 2, then 3, then 4. Each step adds one more.
  2. Doubling. Start with 1, then 2, then 4, then 8. Each step is twice the one before (1, then double to 2, then double to 4). This one grows fast.
  3. Triangle dots. Make dots in triangles: 1 dot, then 3, then 6, then 10. Look at how many you add each time: add 2, then add 3, then add 4. The jump gets one bigger every time.

Ask her: before you build the next step, can you guess how many it has? Build it after to check.

Ask her: in the triangle dots, what comes after 10? (Add 5, so 15.) You found it without building it. That is using the rule.

Game 3: make a pattern, hide the rule

  1. Let her invent a pattern and start the row.
  2. You guess what comes next, and say the rule out loud.
  3. Then swap: you start one, she guesses the rule.

Ask her: can you make a pattern that tricks me? (A tricky one repeats later than you expect, like red, red, red, blue.)

Notice together (no pressure)

  • Every pattern has a rule. Once you find the rule, you can jump ahead and say what comes next without building the whole thing.
  • Repeating patterns come back to the start over and over. Growing patterns get bigger by a rule (add one more, or double, or add a little more each time).
  • The triangle dots 1, 3, 6, 10 are special. They are made of real triangles, and that is exactly what the next module builds.

For the grown-up

  • "How many would step 10 have?" is the seed of algebra. Keep it spoken and physical: build, guess, check. Do not push symbols or formulas. The win is noticing that a rule lets you skip the building.
  • Repeating patterns teach counting by groups (by 2s, by 3s). Growing patterns teach that the amount you add can itself follow a pattern (the triangle dots add 2, 3, 4, 5, ...).
